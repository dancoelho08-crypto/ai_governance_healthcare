# Does any of this work?

A governance tool should be honest about its own evidence base. This page
summarizes what is actually known about whether AI governance frameworks change
anything, and what remains unmeasured.

## External recognition

In June 2026 the WHO Regional Office for Europe published *Bridging theory and
practice — implementation insights on artificial intelligence in health care*,
the report of Working Group 1 of the Strategic Partners' Initiative for Data and
Digital Health (WHO/EURO:2026-13225-52999-82657).

Reviewing more than a dozen reporting and evaluation frameworks, it presents
**four complementary approaches** — and two of them are the two frameworks this
tool implements:

- **OPTICA**, described as a practical evaluation tool for assessing AI solutions
  toward adoption, 77 items in 13 chapters and four domains, requiring input
  from five designated stakeholders.
- **CHAI**, described as a tool for AI solution transparency, whose applied model
  card acts as a standardized "nutrition label" for an AI solution.

The report notes that OPTICA "embeds requirements for organizational AI
governance, which include continuing monitoring and impact assessment."

!!! note "Why this matters for this project"

    WHO placed these two side by side as complementary, not competing. That is
    the premise of the [crosswalk](../crosswalk.md): OPTICA supplies the
    adoption-time depth, CHAI supplies the lifecycle and the transparency
    artifact. Neither subsumes the other.

## What the evidence actually shows

A 2025 scoping review in *JAMIA* — Chan, Rahimi-Ardabilli, Rogers and Coiera,
*The real-world impact of artificial intelligence ethics frameworks across a
decade in healthcare* — looked for studies of frameworks that had actually been
put into practice.

The headline numbers are sobering:

| | |
|---|---|
| Published AI ethics frameworks identified | at least **173** |
| Studies of frameworks actually operationalized | **16** |

Of those 16, the review found three ways frameworks get used: establishing
governance structures (4), pre-implementation ethical review (8), and
post-deployment audit (8).

OPTICA appears among them. Applied to 18 AI solutions at Clalit, it was reported
to produce "improved oversight and accountability for AI use" and "more informed
decisions on AI solution deployment against predefined metrics."

## The gap you should know about

!!! danger "No framework has been shown to improve patient outcomes"

    The review's central finding is blunt:

    > No studies demonstrated a direct link between AIEFs and health-related
    > outcome measures such as patient safety.

    It also notes that **no study reported health-related outcomes such as
    increased quality of care**, and that all 16 used cross-sectional designs
    with no before-and-after comparison.

What that means in practice: completing a checklist in this tool is evidence that
a governance process happened and was documented. It is **not** evidence that
patients are safer. The honest claim for any tool in this category — including
this one — is that it makes decisions legible, attributable and reviewable. The
link from that to clinical benefit is assumed, not demonstrated.

The review's own recommendation is to measure the intermediate stages rather than
claim the endpoint. That is a reasonable thing to ask of your own governance
program too: track whether reviews changed decisions, not merely whether they
were completed.
